What we build

Community engineering, across the systems a place actually needs.

These are not separate service lines. They are one interconnected system, where waste from one becomes the input to another, and the income from that is what makes the service affordable.

Where we work: households, schools, health facilities, churches and faith institutions, community markets, farms and enterprises.

The model

The viability test, applied before we build and not after.

Financial credibility is the foundation for sustainable investment. We apply that test at project level, before construction. Four questions have to hold:

“If those answers do not hold, we say so, and we do not build it.”

Declining unviable work protects the community from a stranded asset, and protects the credibility of everything we do build.

01

Who operates it?

A named person, trained and accountable, identified before handover.

02

Who pays to keep it running?

A service fee the community or institution can genuinely afford.

03

What offsets the cost?

Recovered waste becomes compost, feed or fuel, and that income lowers the fee.

04

What happens if it fails?

A 48-hour response commitment and a published uptime record.

Evidence & accountability

We measure what we build, and publish how it performs.

Most infrastructure has no performance record after handover. We create one as a condition of doing the work, and that record is what makes a service contract, a warranty and an investment case possible at all.

Commissioning baseline

Flow, water quality, energy and output, measured on the day of handover and never reconstructed later.

Service log

Every visit and every fault, with response time and downtime hours recorded.

Published uptime

Performance against a 95% target, reported to the customer twice a year.

Household evidence

Hours returned, money kept, fuel and feed displaced. The numbers a household feels.

Why it matters. Nobody can regulate, finance or scale what nobody is measuring. Our service contracts generate that data site by site, as a by-product of being paid to keep systems running.

Sector alignment

Where our practice meets the continental agenda.

Our model was built independently and maps directly onto the pillars set for Africa's systems transformation in water and sanitation, with comparable alignments in energy access and the circular economy.

Pillar 01

Leadership & framing

Convening, advocacy and the systems framing that connects delivery to policy, with our partner Common Ground Solutions Center.

Pillar 02

Regulation & accountability

Measured baselines and published uptime, the performance data rural regulation currently lacks.

Pillar 03

Professionalisation

Certified makers, trained operators and engineers formed on site, so systems stay repairable within a day's travel.

Pillar 04

Strategic public finance

A viability test before construction, so financial credibility is proven at project level, not assumed.
