How we measure

Service measures and circular measures, published together

We measure what a customer experiences and what the circular model earns, and we publish both. Service measures show whether it works. Circular measures show whether it pays.

Service

Commissioning baseline

Flow, quality, energy and output on the day of handover.

Service log

Every visit and fault, with response time and downtime hours.

Published uptime

Against a 95% target, reported twice a year.

Circular

Waste recovered

Tonnes of manure and organic waste treated, not dumped or burned.

Fee offset

Share of the service cost paid by recovered-product income.

Household gain

Hours returned, money kept, fuel and feed displaced.

Why it matters. Nobody can finance, regulate or scale what nobody measures. The circular measures are what an investor or a circular economist needs to judge whether the model pays.

Where we fit

Built for Uganda’s move from a linear to a circular economy

National plans in Uganda point towards a circular economy. Our work at Nakifuuma is built to line up with them, and to supply the field data behind them.

01

Uganda NDP IV, 2025/26 to 2029/30

Sets industrialisation and household income as priorities and includes promotion of the circular economy.

02

Uganda Circular Economy Roadmap

A national process led by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Cooperatives, backed by the African Development Bank’s Africa Circular Economy Facility.

03

Uganda Green Growth Development Strategy (2017)

The national frame for low-carbon, resource-efficient growth that the roadmap builds on.

04

Africa Circular Economy Facility and Alliance

The African Development Bank’s dedicated trust fund for circular economy innovation, and the government-led alliance it supports.

Also contributes to Sustainable Development Goals 2, 6, 7, 12 and 13.

Sources: NDP IV (UNEP LEAP) · Uganda circular economy roadmap (The Independent) · Africa Circular Economy Facility (AfDB)
