What we do

We design, build, operate and measure

Each system is priced, built and maintained as part of one site, so the products from one lower the cost of another, and the people who run it are trained where it stands.

Integrated community engineering

Six systems, built as one

These are not separate service lines. They are one interconnected system: waste from one becomes the input to another, and the income from that is what makes the service affordable.

Resource recovery

Manure and organic waste into compost, protein feed and fuel briquettes, with barriers to disease.

  • Manure treatment
  • Compost
  • Protein feed
  • Briquettes

Water

Solar pumping, rainwater storage, irrigation and livestock water, with quality tested.

  • Solar pumping
  • Rainwater storage
  • Irrigation
  • Water quality

Renewable energy

Solar power for pumping, processing and cold storage. No tree cutting.

  • Solar generation
  • Productive use
  • Cold storage
  • Backup

Sanitation

Toilets and handwashing where sludge is treated and returned to the soil safely.

  • Toilets
  • Handwashing
  • Sludge treatment
  • Safe reuse

Food systems

Soil health, irrigation and farm infrastructure that let farms grow, store and sell more.

  • Soil health
  • Livestock water
  • Poultry systems
  • Storage

Building and fabrication

Interlocking blocks pressed from local subsoil. No firing, no firewood.

  • Pressed blocks
  • Tanks
  • Dryers
  • Local repair

We work with farms, agri-enterprises, hospitality venues, peri-urban households and markets, and private institutions buying a service.

Circular and regenerative systems

Waste from one system pays for the next

Manure and organic waste are treated safely, turned into products people buy, and that income lowers the fee for water and energy. More food and livestock output means more to recover, and the loop closes.

Nothing useful is wasted. Nothing harmful is passed on.

Recovered, not dumped

Compost, protein feed and fuel briquettes from manure and organic waste.

Safe by design

Barriers to disease built into every recovery step, so reuse does not pass harm on.

Regenerative

Treated sludge and compost returned to the soil. No tree cutting for energy.

Low-carbon building

The viability test

Four questions before we build anything

Before we commit to a build, we ask four questions with the customer. The answers decide whether a system can pay its own way.

01

Who operates it?

A named person, trained and accountable, identified before handover.

02

Who pays to keep it running?

A service fee the customer can genuinely afford, agreed in writing.

03

What offsets the cost?

Recovered products and their market, priced on real local sales.

04

What happens if it fails?

A 48-hour response commitment and a published uptime record.

If they do not hold, we do not build.

A system nobody can pay to run becomes a stranded asset. Saying no early protects the customer and the credibility of everything we do build.

Living labs and learning

We do not produce reports. We produce makers.

A system that can only be repaired by the company that installed it is not a system, it is a dependency. Nakifuuma is our living lab: young people and professionals learn on working systems, with real data, not in a classroom.

Certified makers

Practical courses ending in something built and a certificate: masons, welders, solar technicians, producers.

Trained operators

Every commissioned system is handed to a named, trained operator, recorded on the certificate.

Engineers formed on site

Graduate associates and applied research at a working site: practical formation classrooms do not deliver.

A retention record

We track whether the people we train are still using the skill twelve months on. Training that leads to no work is a cost.

Local enterprise development

If it cannot be repaired locally, the design is not finished

Every system we build creates local work: making, repairing, operating and selling. If a fault cannot be fixed within a day’s travel by someone we trained, we go back to the design.

Local fabrication and repair

Poultry systems, filters, dryers, stalls and tanks made and repaired nearby, so a fault never waits for an import.

Recovered products to sell

Compost, feed, briquettes and pressed blocks, priced on real local sales.

Paid local operators

A service fee the customer can afford, agreed in writing, keeps a trained local operator in work.

Makers into work

Certified masons, welders, solar technicians and producers, building and maintaining the next site.

Is your site ready?

We run the viability test with you, and we only build if a named operator, an affordable fee and a market for recovered products are in place.
