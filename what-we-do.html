What we do

Community engineering, across the systems a place actually needs.

We design, build, commission and maintain. These are not separate service lines. They are one interconnected system.

One system

Waste from one becomes the input to another.

The income from that recovery is what makes each service affordable. This is why we build the systems together instead of one at a time.

The viability test

Applied before we build, not after.

Financial credibility is the foundation for sustainable investment. We apply that test at project level, before construction. Four questions have to hold.

If those answers do not hold, we say so, and we do not build it.

Declining unviable work protects the community from a stranded asset, and protects the credibility of everything we do build.

01

Who operates it?

A named person, trained and accountable, identified before handover.

02

Who pays to keep it running?

A service fee the community or institution can genuinely afford.

03

What offsets the cost?

Recovered waste becomes compost, feed or fuel, and that income lowers the fee.

04

What happens if it fails?

A 48-hour response commitment and a published uptime record.

Capability

We do not produce reports. We produce makers.

A system that can only be repaired by the company that installed it is a dependency, not a system. If it cannot be repaired within a day's travel by someone we trained, the design is not finished.

Certified makers

Practical courses ending in something built and a certificate: masons, welders, solar technicians and producers.

Trained operators

Every commissioned system hands over to a named, trained operator, recorded on the certificate.

Engineers formed on site

Graduate placements and applied research at working sites, the practical formation classrooms do not deliver.

A retention record

We track whether certified makers are still working twelve months on. Training that leads to no work is a cost.
